Losing Is Fun

The line that differentiates a “good” roleplayer and a “great” roleplayer is how you treat other players. Behaving in a way that is respectful and makes the experience better not just selfishly for yourself, but for everyone involved. And sometimes, this can mean letting your character fail and lose on purpose.

You can think of it in writing terms, a story where a character always acts optimally, never makes mistakes, and comes out on top isn’t nearly as satisfying as one where they do make mistakes, get put as a disadvantage, but still manage to scrape by. A good example of this in play for both sides in Space Station is Thieves.

It isn’t uncommon for newer Thief players to make mistakes, put their beacon in an obvious spot, etc. If a Security player thinks they see a beacon within the first 20 minutes of a round, they could confiscate it and get prints off of it and end the Thief’s round there. Or they could pretend to not see it, give the Thief some leeway, let them get further along. If the Thief is given this little grace period early in the shift, they can go on to attempt some objectives, creating more scenarios for people to roleplay around. The CMO’s room gets broken into and the thief is seen. Security gets called in to investigate further.

This extends further to doing other things that put yourself at a disadvantage, and other players in the scene being willing to give you leeway to do so without pouncing on it. For example, stopping in the middle of a fight to trash talk or emote spitting on someone you just stamina crit. And as other participants in the scene, being willing to pause for a second when you see them typing to let them.
