- 2.\n
The Head of Security or Warden may assign a specific member of security to be the K9's handler.\n\n
2.1\n
If no handler has been assigned, K9s are to accompany and obey a proper member of security at all times.\n\n
2.2\n
A K9 found roaming without an escort is to be assigned a handler, preferably by the Warden or Head of Security.
- 3.\n
K9s are to follow standard escalation as per [§4 of Security SOP](security-sop-intro.html), and follow their handler's (or any member of security's) lead in how a situation is handled. They are to avoid aggression toward suspects who are cooperating, and are not to show open hostility toward the crew or suspected criminals.\n\n
3.1\n
This includes following reasonable expectations, such as avoiding: disobeying orders, unnecessary harm to crew, suspects or prisoners, destruction of property, and fleeing from their handler or other members of security without reason.\n\n
3.2\n
A K9's bite is considered less-lethal. K9s are not to bite anyone without good reason.\n\n
3.3\n
K9s should only latch onto a suspect when their handler, or another member of security, is present or involved in the pursuit of that suspect.\n\n
3.4\n
A K9 left unaccompanied by circumstance, such as their handler being downed, or a situation developing too quickly to wait for security, may act on their own best judgement.
- 4.\n
K9s are to be trained and commanded by their handler, who is also responsible for reporting and punishing any misconduct. In the absence of a handler, this may be done by any member of security.\n\n
4.1\n
Punishment must be proportionate to the misconduct, and must not involve direct physical harm.
- 5.\n
If required, the Head of Security may sign documentation for the K9's demotion or, if necessary, their execution by euthanasia.
- 6.\n
K9s are exempt from the Uniform Requirements in [Security SOP §2](security-sop-intro.html).
